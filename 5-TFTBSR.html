Colorado Fourteeners

A short guide to the state’s 14,000-foot peaks, with a data table and a trip planning form.

About the fourteeners

A fourteener is a mountain with a summit at or above 14,000 feet (4,267 m). Colorado has more of them than any other state in the contiguous United States. The Colorado Mountain Club and the U.S. Geological Survey generally count 58 peaks, although the exact total depends on how much a summit must rise above its neighbor to count as a separate mountain.

The highest point in the Rockies

Mount Elbert, in the Sawatch Range, is the tallest peak in Colorado at 14,440 feet. It is also the highest summit in the Rocky Mountains. Its standard routes are non-technical, so it draws a large share of first-time fourteener hikers each summer.

Where the peaks are

Most fourteeners sit in a handful of ranges: the Sawatch Range, the Front Range, the Sangre de Cristo Mountains, the San Juan Mountains, and the Elk Mountains. Peaks near Denver, such as Grays Peak and Mount Bierstadt, are reachable in about an hour and a half by car, which makes them popular for day trips.

Altitude and your body

At 14,000 feet the air holds roughly 40 percent less oxygen per breath than at sea level. Headache, nausea, and dizziness can signal AMS. If symptoms get worse, descend.

Safety basics

A few habits prevent most problems on a fourteener. Open any item below for details.

Start early and watch the weather

Begin hiking before sunrise in summer. Turn around if clouds build or you hear thunder. Exposed ridges and summits are the most dangerous places in a lightning storm.

Carry the essentials

Pack at least two liters of water, extra layers, a headlamp, sun protection, a map, and a whistle. Temperatures at the summit can be 25 degrees Fahrenheit colder than at the trailhead.

Follow Leave No Trace

Stay on the trail to protect fragile alpine tundra. Pack out all trash, and carry out human waste where the land manager requires it.
